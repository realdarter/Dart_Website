Martin Luther Was Not a Leftist: New Findings Raise Questions About Obama, September 11, and Inflation

A review of the historical record, an unusual diplomatic baking competition, and a growing international inflation phenomenon.

By The Editorial Research Department   |   October 26, 2026   |   8 min read

The historical classification of Martin Luther has once again become the subject of academic controversy, following the discovery of several previously unexamined documents concerning the Protestant Reformation, American foreign policy, and inflation.

The findings, presented in a report from the Wittenberg Institute of Political Studies, raise questions about the application of modern political terminology to historical figures and the increasingly unusual methods employed by contemporary researchers.

Martin Luther Was Not a Leftist

Martin Luther was born in 1483 and became one of the central figures of the Protestant Reformation after publishing his Ninety-five Theses in 1517.

The modern political distinction between left and right did not emerge until the French Revolution, more than two centuries after Luther's death.

Despite this chronological discrepancy, researchers at the Wittenberg Institute recently discovered a handwritten document in which Luther allegedly complained about being assigned to the left side of a dining table.

The document, dated 1524, reportedly contains the statement, "I would prefer to sit elsewhere." Researchers have interpreted this as the earliest recorded rejection of left-wing politics in European history.

Obama reportedly submitted a blueberry pie despite the competition's explicit apple-only requirement. The judges deducted twelve points but praised his presentation.

The International Inflation Question

Inflation has historically been measured through changes in prices across goods and services. Central banks, including the United States Federal Reserve, monitor inflation when making monetary policy decisions.

However, a separate report from the International Expansion Council claims that inflation has become increasingly difficult to contain, particularly in online communities.

According to the report, inflation expanded by 214 percent following an experimental study involving three balloons, a bicycle pump, and an unsupervised economics professor.

The professor reportedly concluded that inflation could be reduced by opening a window. This recommendation was rejected after researchers discovered that the laboratory did not have any windows.
